What Is Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)?

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is an estimation of the total caloric energy your body consumes over 24 hours. TDEE is determined by combining your resting metabolism with physical movement and digestion costs.

The Formulas Behind TDEE Calculation

Our baseline engine leverages three scientifically validated equations:

  • Mifflin-St Jeor Equation: Considered the clinical standard for healthy adults without known body fat percentage.
  • Katch-McArdle Equation: Leverages Lean Body Mass (LBM) to deliver heightened accuracy for athletic individuals with known body fat percentage.
  • Revised Harris-Benedict Equation: Historical benchmark formula used for cross-comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE? ▼

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the baseline calories required to survive at rest. TDEE includes your BMR plus all calories burned walking, working, exercising, and digesting food.

How often should I recalculate my TDEE? ▼

Recalculate your TDEE whenever your body weight changes by more than 3–5 kg, or when your job activity/workout routine changes significantly.
